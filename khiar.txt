
Tenor — a repo desk for tokenised securities on Hedera.
Instead of an order book or a lending pool, a lender signs an EIP-712 quote off-chain for a specific security (this is a specials trade, 
not a fungible market) and the borrower executes it in one call: cash moves, both ATS holds get created and the first one executes 
immediately, and the unwind gets handed to the network at open. No keeper, no liquidation bot. At maturity the network calls back with 
nobody online: either the borrower repurchased and gets the collateral back, or they didn't and the lender keeps it. That second branch 
isn't an error path, it's just what a repo does on non-repayment, so there's no price oracle and no margin call, the haircut at open is 
the entire risk control.
What we're using:
- ATS — collateral is a real ERC-3643 security we issued ourselves. ATS ships no identity registry or compliance module (both required, 
neither shipped), so we deployed and wired minimal ones; the permissive testnet default gets swapped for an explicit two-address allowlist
before the demo.
- HIP-1215 — openRepo schedules closeRepo in the same transaction that moves cash and executes the collateral hold, so scheduling isn't a 
separate step, it's inside the atomic open. Two real repos opened and settled end-to-end on testnet today: one defaulted correctly (fired 
255ms after expiry, collateral left locked with the lender, borrower's return hold never executed), one was repaid early and its 
now-orphaned schedule is expected to fire and do nothing. Settlement contract is payer_account_id on both.

non-contract address every time, because forge has to run the script function against its own EVM first to know what to broadcast, and

calldata with cast instead, works fine, just curious if there's a cleaner path.
